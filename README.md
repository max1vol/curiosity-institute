# The Curiosity Institute

The Curiosity Institute is a cozy top-down museum management game where visitors generate the business pressure, reviews shape the design decisions, and short learning games are mandatory gates for progress.

This is not a pure idle builder. Money matters, but coins alone cannot unlock expansion. The player earns the right to grow by proving curator knowledge through small museum-themed challenges.

## Core Loop

1. Open the museum and serve tourists.
2. Earn coins from tickets, exhibits, and optional premium experiences.
3. Read reviews and visitor wishes at the Review Board.
4. Choose one of three guided development options instead of browsing a huge upgrade tree.
5. Pass the required learning checks to unlock the chosen upgrade.
6. Build the new room or feature, attract stronger visitors, and repeat.

That creates a loop with real tension:

- tourists create the money fantasy
- reviews turn feedback into guided choices
- side games matter because they unlock upgrades
- level-ups stay locked until the player demonstrates mastery

## What Makes It Different

- Educational tasks are structural, not optional garnish.
- Visitor reviews keep progression legible and low-overwhelm.
- Upgrades feel earned because business success and curator knowledge both matter.
- The museum gets visually richer as the player learns more.

## Progression Snapshot

### Level 1: Dusty Foyer

Open rooms:

- Ticket Desk
- Coat Hooks
- Review Board
- Curator Desk

Locked:

- Roman Gallery
- Donation Urn
- Coin Mint Demonstration
- Map Room

Starting resources:

- Coins: 150
- Timber: 12
- Stone: 12
- Glass: 4
- Ink: 2

Level 2 requires:

- Serve 15 tourists
- Reach 3.5-star average review
- Open 1 exhibition
- Earn 250 total coins
- Pass Curator Check A

The key rule is simple: the museum cannot expand until a real exhibition is running and the curator has demonstrated knowledge.

## Design Pillars

### 1. Reviews Create Guided Choice

The game does not dump twenty upgrades on the player. At the end of the day, the Review Board offers three responses to what visitors actually asked for.

Example branch after Day 1:

- Coin Mint Demonstration: best for extra income
- Label Stand Upgrade: best for higher ratings
- Lantern Cases: best for longer visit time

### 2. Learning Gates Unlock Construction

Each meaningful upgrade is tied to one or more short side games:

- estimation
- match pairs
- multiple choice
- free text
- true or false
- fill the gap

If the player does not pass the required checks, the build button stays locked.

### 3. Expansion Feels Like Earning Accreditation

Even a profitable museum cannot brute-force level progression. The player has to show understanding of labels, replicas, visitor care, and object interpretation before the institution expands.

These are not abstract quizzes. They are framed as curator work, desk tasks, accreditation checks, and exhibit-planning moments inside the museum fantasy.

The estimation screen supports prompts like "How long was Hadrian's Wall?" and rewards the player with permits, materials, and confidence boosts.

The pair-matching screen turns object literacy into a tactile museum task. It works well for mosaics, helmets, aqueducts, amphitheatres, labels, and replica handling.

## Example Play Flow

### Before Opening

The player starts in the Dusty Foyer, sees the Roman Gallery locked, and learns that two permits are required:

- History Permit
- Exhibit Planning Permit

Both permits come from side games, not from grinding coins.

### Day 1

- The player passes an estimation check and a match-pairs check.
- The Roman Gallery is restored.
- Tourists enter, buy tickets, and visit the first exhibition.
- Reviews ask for interactivity, clearer labels, and more excitement.

### Day 2

- The Review Board offers three guided responses.
- The player chooses Coin Mint Demonstration.
- A multiple-choice task and a short written response unlock the build.
- Visitor spending increases, reviews improve, and the museum becomes visibly busier.

### Level Up

Even after strong income and reviews, the player still has to pass Curator Check A before the museum expands. Once that check is passed, the Map Gallery branch opens and the museum gains a new thematic lane.

## Why This Version Works

- The player fantasy is "I am earning the right to build a better museum."
- Reviews create direction without removing agency.
- Learning tasks stay short, specific, and clearly tied to museum decisions.
- New rooms and developments are visibly connected to what tourists asked for.
- Progress feels deserved because both care and competence are required.

## Asset Workflow

The concept art in this repository was generated with Nano Banana using a reference-first workflow:

1. Generate a stable curator and foyer anchor.
2. Reuse those images as input for later scene generation.
3. Validate outputs with fresh subagents to catch composition drift and incorrect unlock states.
4. Curate only the images that clearly communicate progression.

The local helper used for this workflow lives at `scripts/render-concept-art.mjs`.
